## Read by us (verified)

- **Annual Report v. 67 (1920-21), p. 52** (HathiTrust uc1.b2987052, seq 60; in-item search in Chrome, Nov 1):
  "the ten precincts in which the Board divided the school"; notices "printed by the Blewett print shop, were served on
  the sixty judges and clerks, all of whom appeared before the Board of Election Commissioners the following morning
  for the purpose of being qualified and taking oath of office"; judges and clerks "instructed in the duties of their
  new offices by two Blewett patrons who were precinct committeemen"; "The polls were open for only a half hour before
  school, a half hour after school and during the lunch period"; registration "Friday October 22nd and Monday, October
  25th"; "Twelve hundred fifty or 90% of the student body registered"; "the Art Department made posters, poll books,
  and tally sheets"; "the Manual Training Department made the frame work for 30 booths which were covered and provided
  with pencils by the Civics Department"; "the Junior Life Staff offered to make the issue of October 28 an Election
  Issue"; "the Printing Department printed enough "I have voted" tags to give each student one as he voted"; "the study
  hall pupils cut into strips 1400 ballot sheets". The report calls the school "Blewett" only.
- **Full name and principal**: Harvard College Class of 1906, Fifteenth Anniversary Report (1921), Philip W. L. Cox
  entry (archive.org n4reportclass1906harvuoft): "in September, 1917 I became principal of the Ben Blewett Junior High
  School, St. Louis; in June, 1920 I became headmaster of the Washington School of New York." Also lists his article
  "The Ben Blewett Junior High School: An Experiment in Democracy," School Review, May, 1919.
- **Federal bibliography**: Bureau of Education Library Leaflet No. 20, List of References on the Junior High School
  (govinfo GOVPUB-I16-8f9fa4ec41144678e353945ec5542809): "Cox, Philip W. L. The Ben Blewett junior high school : an
  experiment in democracy. School review, 27: 345-59, May 1919."
- **Tennessee Williams**: Mark Cave, Tennessee Williams Annual Review 7 (2005), on the Historic New Orleans Collection
  holdings, para. 27: "beginning with The Blewett Junior Life Yearbook for 1925, Williams's junior high school
  yearbook—and contain an early Williams poem entitled "Demon Smoke.""
- **Building history** (secondary, Built St. Louis): Smith Academy and Manual Training School built 1905; "Ben Blewett
  Junior High School, 1918, a public school"; Blewett High School 1931; Harris Teachers College 1949; closed 1994.
