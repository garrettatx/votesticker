## Election-week facts voters miss

All sources in this section are official **`.gov`** sources. Dates translated from statutory intervals are explicitly identified.

### Name missing from the list

**Ask workers to check name variations and registration records before leaving.** Florida’s polling-place manual provides an escalation process, including provisional voting when eligibility cannot be resolved. [files.floridados](https://files.floridados.gov/media/708161/final-ds-de-11-manual.pdf)

**Exact sentences:**
> “If the voter’s name is not on the precinct register, check for name variations.”

> “If the voter’s name or address has not changed, contact the Supervisor’s office or, if available at the precinct, check the master list of registered voters.”

> “If you still are not able to find the voter’s name or cannot get in contact with the Supervisor of Elections’ office to determine more about the voter’s eligibility, follow the procedure in Section B. Provisional Ballot (Chapter V).”

**Official URL:**  
[https://files.floridados.gov/media/708161/final-ds-de-11-manual.pdf](https://files.floridados.gov/media/708161/final-ds-de-11-manual.pdf)  
See Chapter IV, “Name on Precinct Register.” The document is effective July 2024, rather than newly issued for 2026.

### A provisional ballot needs follow-up

**Keep the written instructions given to you.** They must explain how to check whether your ballot counted and what information you need to access its status. [flsenate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)

**Exact sentences:**
> “Each person casting a provisional ballot shall be given written instructions regarding the person’s right to provide the supervisor with written evidence of his or her eligibility to vote and regarding the free access system established pursuant to subsection (7).”

> “The instructions must contain the supervisor’s contact information along with information on how to access the system and the information the voter will need to provide to obtain information on his or her particular ballot.”

> “Information regarding provisional ballots shall be available no later than 30 days following the election.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)  
See subsections (5) and (7).

### Eligibility evidence: November 5

For the November 3 election, the statutory second-day deadline is **Thursday, November 5, at 5 p.m.** This is a deadline to provide supporting evidence—not a promise that a provisional ballot will count. [flsenate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)

**Exact sentence:**
> “A person casting a provisional ballot has the right to present written evidence supporting his or her eligibility to vote to the supervisor of elections by not later than 5 p.m. on the second day following the election.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)

### No ID has a special rule

A provisional ballot cast **solely because acceptable photo/signature ID was missing** does not automatically require a later trip to show ID. Other eligibility and signature requirements still apply.

**Exact sentence:**
> “If you voted a provisional ballot solely because you did not have an acceptable photo and signature identification, you do not need to provide further evidence of your eligibility in order for your ballot to count, provided you are otherwise eligible and qualified to vote.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://dos.fl.gov/elections/contacts/frequently-asked-questions/faq-voting/](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/contacts/frequently-asked-questions/faq-voting/)  
See question 8.

### Wrong precinct is different

Do not assume a provisional ballot makes the wrong Election Day precinct harmless: the canvassing board checks entitlement to vote **at that precinct**. [flsenate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)

**Exact sentence:**
> “The county canvassing board shall examine each Provisional Ballot Voter’s Certificate and Affirmation to determine if the person voting that ballot was entitled to vote at the precinct where the person cast a vote in the election and that the person had not already cast a ballot in the election.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/101.048)

### Signature cures: November 5

**November 4 is still an action day:** voters with missing or mismatched signatures should complete the cure process before **5 p.m. Thursday, November 5**. Both mail and provisional ballots have signature-cure procedures. [votebrevard](https://www.votebrevard.gov/Election-Information/Provisional-Ballots/Provisional-Ballot-Signature-Cure-Affidavit-Instructions-and-Form)

**Exact mail-ballot heading:**
> “The deadline for Mail Ballot signature cures for the 2026 General Election is Thursday, November 5, 2026 at 5:00 p.m.”

**Exact sentence:**
> “Your ballot cannot be counted unless we receive your signed affidavit and a copy of your identification by 5:00 p.m., the 2nd day after the election.”

**Official mail-ballot URL:**  
[https://www.votebrevard.gov/Ballots-by-Mail/Mail-Ballot-Signature-Cure-Affidavit-Instructions-and-Form](https://www.votebrevard.gov/Ballots-by-Mail/Mail-Ballot-Signature-Cure-Affidavit-Instructions-and-Form)

**Official provisional-ballot URL:**  
[https://www.votebrevard.gov/Election-Information/Provisional-Ballots/Provisional-Ballot-Signature-Cure-Affidavit-Instructions-and-Form](https://www.votebrevard.gov/Election-Information/Provisional-Ballots/Provisional-Ballot-Signature-Cure-Affidavit-Instructions-and-Form)

These county pages implement the deadline; voters should submit their documents to **their own county’s** supervisor.

### Late domestic mail does not count

**A November 3 postmark is not enough for an ordinary domestic ballot.** The receipt deadline and the later signature-cure deadline are different.

**Exact sentences:**
> “A returned voted ballot must be received, regardless of postmark, by the Supervisor of Elections' office no later than 7:00 pm (local time) on Election Day.”

> “Untimely received ballots are otherwise not counted.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/)  
See “What is the Deadline to Return a Vote-by-Mail Ballot?”

### Overseas mail has an exception

The qualifying overseas-mail extension runs through **November 13** for this election; it is not a general extension for domestic voters.

**Exact sentence:**
> “The mailed overseas voter’s vote-by-mail ballot must be postmarked or dated by Election Day and received within 10 days of the election in order to be counted, provided the ballot is otherwise proper.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/military-and-overseas-citizens-voting/](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/military-and-overseas-citizens-voting/)

### Later counting is scheduled

**Counting after election night is not the same as accepting late domestic ballots.** Brevard’s published schedule illustrates the distinction: provisional/mail review follows Election Day, and qualifying overseas ballots have a later review.

**Exact schedule entries—not prose sentences:**
> “Review of Mail and Provisional Ballots, Certify 1st Unofficial Results (if a recount is not required) - Friday, November 6, 2026 at 1:00 p.m.”

> “Review 10-day Count of Overseas Mail Ballots & Certify Official Results - Friday, November 13, 2026 at 2:00 p.m.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.votebrevard.gov/News-and-Notices/Election-Public-Notices/notice-of-2026-general-election-canvassing-board-schedule](https://www.votebrevard.gov/News-and-Notices/Election-Public-Notices/notice-of-2026-general-election-canvassing-board-schedule)

**Scope:** Those meeting dates are **Brevard-specific**, not a statewide meeting calendar. The notice was updated October 28, 2026.

### Requested mail, now voting in person

Requesting a mail ballot does not itself prevent in-person voting. Whether it has already been received determines the next step.

**Exact sentences:**
> “If the ballot has not been received, the voter will be allowed to vote a regular ballot.”

> “If it cannot be determined if the ballot has been received, the voter will be allowed to vote a provisional ballot.”

> “If the ballot has been received, the ballot is deemed cast and the voter to have already voted.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/)  
See “How to Vote in Person if Ballot Requested.”

### Emergency mail-ballot pickup

Last-minute pickup is not an unrestricted alternative to appearing at the polls: an emergency affidavit applies during the specified period.

**Exact sentences:**
> “If a voter or designee waits until the start of mandatory early voting or up through Election Day to pick up or have delivered a vote-by-mail ballot, a special affidavit must also be completed.”

> “The voter must affirm in the affidavit that an emergency exists that keeps the voter from being able to vote at a designated early voting site in his or her county or at his or her assigned polling place on Election Day.”

**Official URL:**  
[https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/)  
See “Who Can Pick Up a Vote-by-Mail Ballot?”
