## What the research establishes

### Stickers and voting intentions

A **2025 peer-reviewed study in *Political Communication*** compares “I Voted” and “I Will Vote” stickers, but its reported outcome is **voting intention**, not verified turnout. [forthrightaccess](https://www.forthrightaccess.com/publications/)

**Exact university sentence:**
> “The researchers ran two complementary experiments about the influence of “I Voted” and “I Will Vote” stickers on voting intentions among U.S. adults who did not vote in previous presidential elections.”

**University source:**  
[https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-appc-i-will-vote-using-future-oriented-frames-motivate-voters](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-appc-i-will-vote-using-future-oriented-frames-motivate-voters)

**Journal DOI:**  
[https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2025.2472768](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2025.2472768)

The reported advantage is for **“I Will Vote,” not “I Voted.”** It should not be presented as proof that distributing ordinary stickers increases actual turnout. [penntoday.upenn](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-appc-i-will-vote-using-future-oriented-frames-motivate-voters)
