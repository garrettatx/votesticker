Where You Can Vote
Any voter can vote early, no excuse needed, at the early voting location in the county where you're registered. On Election Day, vote at your assigned polling place, or your ballot won't count.
The OK Voter Portal shows your polling place, your early voting location and your sample ballot. All you need is your name and date of birth.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
A photo ID from the U.S. government, the State of Oklahoma or a federally recognized tribe, with your name, your photo and an expiration date after Election Day
The free voter identification card your County Election Board mailed you when you registered
A photo ID can't expire before Election Day, unless it's valid indefinitely. The voter ID card from your county has no photo and still counts.
Don't leave. Ask for a provisional ballot and sign a sworn affidavit. After Election Day, your county checks your name, address, date of birth and license number or last four Social Security digits against your registration, and your ballot counts if they match.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered Oklahoma voter can vote absentee by mail. You don't need a reason.
Get the affidavit on a standard absentee ballot notarized. Notaries can't charge for it, and many banks, credit unions and libraries do it free. Physically incapacitated and blind voters need two witnesses instead.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign gear at home. Don't wear or bring anything that advocates for or against a candidate or issue on the ballot: electioneering within 300 feet of a polling place or early voting entrance, or of anyone in line outside, is a crime. Notes and a marked sample ballot are fine if you keep them out of view until you're in the booth.
Wait to post. You can take a picture of your marked ballot, but don't share it until you're outside the election enclosure, the area where voters check in, get ballots and vote.
In line at 7 p.m.? Stay in line. Every eligible voter in line by 7 p.m. gets to vote.

What is a provisional ballot in Oklahoma, and does it count?
You can ask for a provisional ballot if you don't have a valid ID, your name isn't in the precinct registry, or you think your voter information is wrong. Before results are certified, your County Election Board checks your registration, and your ballot counts if your eligibility is verified.

Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
