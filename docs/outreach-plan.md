# Outreach and Press Plan

Started October 31, 2026, from a Perplexity review Garrett shared. This is the plan for the outreach part of the
visibility strategy (`docs/visibility-strategy-20261026.md`). Every send, post and purchase needs Garrett's OK first.

## Why Outreach Matters Here

On October 31, ChatGPT answered "where can I find a virtual I voted sticker" with Portage MI, the Washington
Secretary of State, Canva and FVAP, not VoteSticker.org. Its crawler can reach the site, so access isn't the
problem. The site is about two weeks old and has almost no links or mentions, so search engines and AI answers
don't trust it yet. Wording fixes help (the home page digital-sticker answer, queued after pass 8), but trust comes
from other sites linking to and writing about this one.

## What We Keep From the Review

- **Lead with what's true and useful**: free digital "I voted" stickers for every state and DC, no account or
  email, nothing to buy, made in the browser, with links to official election offices. No claims about raising
  turnout: the site's own FAQ says the research doesn't support one.
- **Narrow privacy wording**: say "no account" and "made in your browser". Don't say "no tracking", because the
  site uses Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics and says so.
- **Say what the site isn't**: not a government site, and the stickers aren't official or proof of voting.
- **Personal pitches over wire blasts**: a short note to a reporter who covers the topic beats a mass release.
- **The release lives on our own site first**, and anything else points back to it.
- **No faked mentions**: no planted forum posts, no paid "citation campaigns".

## What We Change or Drop

- **Paid wire services are last, not first.** Paid releases ($75 to $150 from PR Underground or EIN Presswire,
  $399 from eReleases) mostly republish on low-traffic news-feed sites. Those links usually carry no ranking
  weight, and republishing isn't a reporter writing a story. One free PRLog post is fine as a backstop. Spend
  money only if Garrett wants it, after the personal pitches.
- **A better hook than "we launched a site."** The history page has original research no one else has
  published: "I have voted" tags back to a 1920 St. Louis Board of Education report, earlier than the 1980s
  stories most articles repeat. "Where the I voted sticker came from" is a story a reporter can write. "A website
  offers stickers" mostly isn't.
- **Timing follows the news cycle.** Two days before an election, newsrooms are full. Three windows work better:
  1. **Election week (Nov 2 to 4):** the most a pitch can do is land in a useful-resources roundup ("voted by mail?
     here's a sticker"). One or two pitches at most, and only to people already writing that roundup.
  2. **After the election (Nov 4 to 15):** the history angle and the 2026 sticker-contest designs, as feature
     material.
  3. **The 2028 run-up (summer 2027 on):** the real launch, with a year of history, the sticker archive and
     state contest coverage behind it. Most of the effort goes here.

## Who to Pitch

Real coverage areas only, each pitch written for that person:

- Austin: KUT, Austin American-Statesman, KXAN, Austin Monitor, Community Impact (built in Austin).
- Design and civic tech: writers who have covered "I voted" sticker contests (search each state's 2026
  contest coverage; those reporters already care).
- Election office communications staff whose official digital stickers we link to (Washington SOS, Oregon SOS,
  Multnomah County, Grand Rapids, Ann Arbor, Portage). This isn't asking for a link. It's telling them we credit
  their sticker, and asking them to flag anything wrong.
- Librarians and historians for the 1920 find (St. Louis Public Library, the Missouri Historical Society).
- Sites Garrett controls: garrettn.com and resistnow (resistnow already links from four voting pages).

4. Contact line: Garrett's name and the Garrett Digital email he chooses. No placeholders go out.
5. Add a short Press page or section on the site that the release links to (after Nov 4; no new pages before the

## Draft Release (for Garrett's Edit)

**VoteSticker.org Offers Free Digital "I Voted" Stickers for Every State**

*Austin-built, nonpartisan site pairs shareable sticker designs with links to official voting information.*

AUSTIN, Texas: [date]. Voted by mail or early, or want an "I voted" sticker for your profile picture?
VoteSticker.org offers free digital designs for all 50 states and Washington, D.C., in English and Spanish.

Garrett Digital, an Austin search marketing consultancy, built the independent, nonpartisan site. Visitors pick
their state, choose a ready-made design or make their own, and save the image. There's no account, no email
address and nothing to buy. Custom stickers are made in the visitor's browser, not uploaded.

Options include "I voted," "I voted early," "I voted by mail," and first-time voter designs. They're sized for the
profile-picture circle, and a transparent PNG can go over a photo or into a story.

Each state page lists that state's 2026 voting dates from official sources and links to its election offices. The
site also points to official digital stickers from election offices and tells the stories behind state and local
sticker designs, including the 2026 contest winners. Its history page traces "I have voted" tags back to 1920.

VoteSticker.org isn't a government website, and its designs aren't official stickers or proof of voting. Voters
should confirm registration, polling places, hours, ID rules and deadlines with their state or local election
office.

https://votesticker.org
