## Questions People Ask
 Where can I get an I voted sticker?
 Poll workers at most polling places and vote centers hand you one after you vote. Each state or county picks its own design. Voted by mail? Some offices put a sticker in the ballot packet or post a digital one (see the list above). Or save a free one here, a classic one or one made for your state. Your state page links to its official ballot tracker where there is one, so you can check that your ballot arrived.
 Is there a digital or virtual I voted sticker?
 Yes. Vote Sticker has free digital I voted stickers for every state and DC, with no account needed. Each one saves as a PNG for Instagram, Facebook, a story or your profile picture. Turn on Cut out the circle in the maker for see-through corners. Some election offices post their own digital stickers too. The ones we found are under Official Digital Stickers From Election Offices.
 How do I make it my profile picture?
 Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
 When should I switch to I voted?
 After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
 Do I voted stickers get more people to vote?
 The research gives no simple yes or no. A 2025 study found that seeing an "I Will Vote" sticker raised people’s plans to vote more than an "I Voted" sticker did, but it measured plans, not votes. A 2010 Facebook experiment with 61 million people found a small rise in voter turnout when people saw friends who said they had voted. That was a message inside Facebook, not a profile picture. That’s why the maker has messages for before you vote, like Vote early and Vote Nov 3, as well as I voted for after.
 Is it really free?
 Yes. There's nothing to buy, no account and no email list. Garrett Digital built it as a public service and pays for the hosting.
 Do you keep my information?
 Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
 Is this an official election site?
 No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For voter registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
 Where do the dates come from?
 From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
 Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
 In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
 What if my state doesn't have early voting?
 Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
 Can I post a photo of my ballot?
 It depends on the state. Some states allow a picture of your own marked ballot, and some make it illegal to show one. Each state page says what that state allows, where we could confirm it. The stickers made here show no ballot.

## Where You Can Vote
 During early voting you can vote at any early voting location in the county where you live. On Election Day, it depends on your county: counties in the Countywide Polling Place Program let you vote at any location in the county, and the rest send you to your assigned precinct.
 My Voter Portal shows your registration and where you can vote. Your county elections office lists early voting sites and their hours.

## What ID to Bring
 Bring one of these:
 Texas driver license
 Texas Election Identification Certificate
 Texas personal identification card
 Texas handgun license
 U.S. military ID card with your photo
 U.S. citizenship certificate with your photo
 U.S. passport, book or card
 If you are 18 to 69, your ID can be expired up to four years. If you are 70 or older, it can be expired for any length of time. The citizenship certificate doesn't expire.
 Don't have one of these and can't reasonably get one? You can still vote a regular ballot. Bring one supporting document, such as your voter registration certificate, a certified birth certificate, or a current utility bill, bank statement, paycheck, government check or other government document with your name and an address. Then sign a short form called a Reasonable Impediment Declaration.

## Who Can Vote by Mail
 You can vote by mail in Texas if you:
 are 65 or older on Election Day
 are sick or disabled
 are expected to give birth within three weeks before or after Election Day
 will be away from your county on Election Day and during early voting hours
 are confined in jail or involuntary civil commitment but otherwise eligible
 If someone helps you with your mail ballot, their name and address go on the carrier envelope and they sign it.
 Texas has no drop boxes. Your marked ballot can go back by mail, by a delivery company, or by you in person to the early voting clerk's office, only while polls are open on Election Day and with an accepted ID. No one else can drop it off for you. Changed your mind? Bring the mail ballot with you and hand it in at a polling place so you can vote in person. Without it, you can vote only a provisional ballot.
 Missing signature, a signature that doesn't match, or missing ID or witness information? You'll get a notice with a corrective action form on the back. Return it by mail or in person, or fix an ID number online in the Ballot by Mail Tracker, by Monday, November 9.

## Returning Your Ballot
 Every active registered voter gets a ballot in the mail with a postage-paid return envelope. Didn't get yours, or it got lost or damaged? In the 30 Voter's Choice Act counties, any vote center in your county can give you a replacement ballot. In other counties, ask your county elections office.
 Sign and date the outside of the return envelope, with a signature that matches your California driver's license, state ID or registration. Can't return it yourself? Fill out the authorization on the envelope and anyone can take it in for you, as long as they aren't paid per ballot.
 Drop it at any polling place or ballot drop-off location in California by 8 p.m. on Election Day. Drop boxes are clearly marked "Official Ballot Drop Box."
 If you forgot to sign or your signature doesn't compare, your county will reach you by mail, phone, email or Where's My Ballot? and send a form. Fill it out and return it by 5 p.m. on November 25.

## Questions People Ask
 Answers come from official California sources. Last checked October 31, 2026.
 When is the ballot deadline in California?
 Every active registered voter gets a ballot by mail. Mail it postmarked by November 3 so it arrives within seven days, or drop it off by 8 p.m. that day. Polling places and vote centers are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. on November 3.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 Do I need ID to vote in California?
 Usually not. Most California voters don't show ID. Some first-time voters who registered without an ID number may be asked for one, such as a driver's license, passport, or utility bill.
 From vigarchive.sos.ca.gov.
 Can I still register to vote in California?
 Yes. The regular deadline was October 19, 2026. You can still register and vote the same day at your county elections office, a vote center, or a polling place, through Election Day.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 Can I take a picture of my ballot in California?
 Yes. Since 2017, California law lets you photograph your own ballot and share it. You can't photograph other voters going in or out.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 What is a provisional ballot in California, and does it count?
 If your name isn't on the list, you can still vote with a provisional ballot. It counts if elections officials find you're eligible. Registering the same day also means voting a provisional ballot.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 What if I am in line when the polls close in California?
 You can still vote. California's Voter Bill of Rights gives you the right to vote if you're still in line when the polls close.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 How do I track my mail ballot in California?
 Sign up for Where's My Ballot? at WheresMyBallot.sos.ca.gov to get text, email or voice call updates when your ballot is mailed, received and counted, or if there's a problem to fix.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in California?
 Your county will contact you and send a form to sign. Return it by 5 p.m. on November 25, 2026, or your ballot won't count. Fill it out right away.
 From the California Secretary of State.
 Is it too late to mail my ballot in California?
 A mailed ballot counts if it's postmarked by November 3 and arrives within 7 days. Mailing it in the last week? Take it to a post office and ask a postal employee for a hand postmark, or use a drop box.
 From the California Secretary of State.
