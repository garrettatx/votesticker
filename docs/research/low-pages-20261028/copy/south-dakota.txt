When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote Early
In South Dakota, any registered voter can vote an absentee ballot in person at the county auditor's office, from Friday, September 18, through Monday, November 2. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
On November 2, the office is open until 5 p.m. or its regular closing time, whichever is later.
Election Day hours: Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time. South Dakota has two time zones.
ID: You'll be asked for a photo ID, such as a South Dakota driver's license. Without one, you can sign an affidavit and still vote a regular ballot.

Where You Can Vote
Want to vote before Election Day? Go to your County Auditor's office once absentee voting begins and vote in person. Bring a photo ID, or sign a personal identification affidavit and vote a regular ballot.
Find your polling place in the state's Voter Information Portal. It's also on the acknowledgement notice your county sent when you registered. Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time.
You're not registered until your county election official accepts your application and sends you an acknowledgement notice. That notice also lists your polling place. No notice within 15 days? Contact your county election official.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
South Dakota driver's license or nondriver ID card
U.S. government photo ID, such as a passport
U.S. Armed Forces ID
Current student photo ID from a South Dakota high school or accredited college or university
Tribal photo ID
No photo ID? Sign a personal identification affidavit, and you still vote a regular ballot.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign signs and posters at home. Within 100 feet of any entrance to a polling place, including where people vote absentee, no one may display campaign posters, signs or other campaign materials or ask for votes.
Take your sticker selfie, not a ballot selfie. South Dakota law bars showing anyone your marked ballot in a way that reveals your votes.
If you're at your polling place, inside or outside, before the polls close, stay. You can still vote.

Questions People Ask
Answers come from official South Dakota sources. Last checked October 26, 2026.
When are polls open in South Dakota on Election Day?
Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time. South Dakota has two time zones.
From the South Dakota Secretary of State .
Do I need ID to vote in South Dakota?
You'll be asked for a photo ID, such as a South Dakota driver's license. Without one, you can sign an affidavit and still vote a regular ballot.
From the South Dakota Secretary of State .
Can I still register to vote in South Dakota?
No, not for the November 3 election. Your signed form had to reach your county auditor by October 19, 2026, and South Dakota has no Election Day registration.
From the South Dakota Secretary of State .
Can I take a picture of my ballot in South Dakota?
No. South Dakota law bars making a marked ballot public in a way that reveals the votes on it, and a photo you share would do that.
From South Dakota law .
When is the mail ballot deadline in South Dakota?
Your county election official must get your ballot on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026, in time to send it to your precinct before polls close at 7 p.m. local time. A ballot that arrives after the polls close isn't counted.
From the South Dakota Secretary of State .
What is a provisional ballot in South Dakota, and does it count?
A provisional ballot counts only if your county finds you registered in that precinct before the registration deadline. The county checks before the official canvass.
From South Dakota law .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in South Dakota?
Running late? Hand it to your county election official on Election Day early enough for it to reach your precinct before 7 p.m. local time. Ballots delivered after the polls close aren't counted.
From South Dakota law .
How do I make it my profile picture?
Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
