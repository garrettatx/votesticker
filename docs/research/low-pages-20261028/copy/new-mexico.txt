When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote Early
Early voting in New Mexico runs Tuesday, October 6 to Saturday, October 31. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
Early voting starts at county clerk offices. More sites open across the state on October 17.
Election Day hours: Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Mailed ballots must arrive by 7 p.m. on Election Day.
ID: Usually not. You state your name, year of birth, and registered address. First-time voters who registered by mail without ID must show one, such as a photo ID or a utility bill.

Where You Can Vote
Vote early in person at your County Clerk's office starting October 6. More early voting sites open across the state on October 17, and early voting ends Saturday, October 31. Check your County Clerk for sites and hours.
Look up early voting sites in the state's Voter Information Portal, or call your County Clerk for sites, dates and hours. Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Election Day.
Registering or updating your registration the same day you vote? Bring a photo ID from the federal government, a state, a tribe, nation or pueblo, or a school. If it doesn't show your current address, also bring a utility bill, bank statement, paycheck or similar document from the last 90 days with your name and address.

What ID to Bring
Most voters don't show ID. You state your name, year of birth and registered address. If you registered by mail for the first time and didn't send ID, bring a photo ID or a document with your name and current address, or you'll vote a provisional ballot.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered voter can ask for a mailed ballot. You can also join the permanent absentee list and get one for every statewide election.
Mail it back, postage prepaid, or hand it in at your County Clerk's office. Only you, a caregiver or an immediate family member (spouse, child, parent, brother or sister) can deliver it.

When are polls open in New Mexico on Election Day?
Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Mailed ballots must arrive by 7 p.m. on Election Day.

Do I need ID to vote in New Mexico?
Usually not. You state your name, year of birth, and registered address. First-time voters who registered by mail without ID must show one, such as a photo ID or a utility bill.
From the New Mexico Secretary of State .
Can I still register to vote in New Mexico?
Yes. Regular registration closed October 6, 2026, but you can register and vote the same day at your County Clerk's office, some early voting sites, or any polling place in your county on Election Day.
From the New Mexico Secretary of State .
When is the mail ballot deadline in New Mexico?
Your ballot must reach your County Clerk, or your precinct, by 7 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Return postage is prepaid, but leave enough time for it to arrive.
From the New Mexico Secretary of State .
What is a provisional ballot in New Mexico, and does it count?
You get a provisional ballot if your name isn't on the roster at your polling place, or if you're a first-time voter who registered by mail and doesn't bring the required ID. Check its status after the election in the state's provisional ballot lookup.
From the New Mexico Secretary of State .
How do I track my mail ballot in New Mexico?
Check the status of your absentee application and ballot with the Secretary of State's ballot tracker, or contact your County Clerk's office.

How do I make it my profile picture?
Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
