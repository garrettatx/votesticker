When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote on Election Day
Alabama doesn’t have early voting open to everyone. Most voters vote on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot.
Election Day hours: Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Some polling places in the Eastern Time Zone keep those hours in Eastern Time.
ID: Yes. Bring a photo ID, such as an Alabama driver's license, a U.S. passport, or a free Alabama Photo Voter ID card. Without one, you can vote a regular ballot if two poll workers can identify you.

Where You Can Vote
Alabama has no early voting, so most people vote at their polling place on Election Day. If you qualify for an absentee ballot, you can also visit your county's Absentee Election Manager to ask for one. In most counties that's the circuit clerk.
Your voter ID card, a postcard from your county Board of Registrars, lists your precinct and polling place. You can also look it up with My Voting Information on the Secretary of State's site.
Just registered? Watch for a voter ID postcard from your county Board of Registrars. That's your sign you're registered. Not sure? Call your Board of Registrars or check at alabamavotes.gov.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Alabama driver's license, including the ALEA Digital Driver's License
Alabama non-driver ID, including the ALEA Digital Non-Driver ID
Free Alabama Photo Voter ID card
U.S. passport
Military ID
Tribal ID
Employee ID from the federal government, the State of Alabama, or an Alabama county, city, board or other entity of the state
Student or employee ID from a public or private college or university in Alabama
Student or employee ID from a state college or university in any other state
Any other federal or state-issued photo ID, such as a pistol permit with a photo
An Alabama driver's license or non-driver ID still works if it expired less than 60 days ago. The other IDs on the list must be valid.
No photo ID? You can still vote a regular ballot if your name is on the voter list and two election officials identify you. If they can't, you vote a provisional ballot and have until 5 p.m. on Friday, November 6, 2026, to bring a photo ID to your county Board of Registrars. Need an ID? The Board of Registrars gives out the Alabama Photo Voter ID card for free.

Who Can Vote by Mail
You can vote absentee in Alabama if you:
expect to be away from your county on Election Day
are ill or have a physical disability that keeps you from getting to the polling place
are 65 or older or have a disability, and a physical condition keeps you from reaching your assigned polling place
are a registered Alabama voter living outside your county, such as a member of the armed forces, someone working outside the U.S., a college student, or the spouse or child of one
are an appointed election officer or poll watcher at a polling place other than your own
expect to work a required shift of 10 hours or more during polling hours
are a caregiver for a close family member who is confined to their home
are in prison or jail and haven't been convicted of a felony involving moral turpitude
Sign the affidavit on your ballot envelope in front of a notary or two witnesses who are 18 or older. Without that, the ballot can't be counted. Online or remote notarizing isn't allowed.
Send your ballot by U.S. Mail or a commercial carrier, or hand it yourself to your county's Absentee Election Manager. Those are the only legal ways to return it.
Check the affidavit envelope before you seal it: your signature, and two witnesses or a notary. Alabama has no way to count a ballot with a deficient envelope.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
You can wear campaign buttons or T-shirts into the polling place in Alabama. Just don't hang around or leave campaign materials behind. Campaigners have to stay at least 30 feet from the entrance.
Bring your phone, just don't disturb other voters. Inside, the only photo allowed is of or with your own ballot, so take your sticker selfie outside.
Get in line before 7 p.m. Anyone who tries to join the line after 7 p.m. can't vote.

Can I take a picture of my ballot in Alabama?
Yes, you can take a photo of your own ballot. Other photos inside the polling place are not allowed.

When is the mail ballot deadline in Alabama?
A mailed ballot must reach your county's Absentee Election Manager by noon on Tuesday, November 3. Bringing it yourself? Get it to that office by 5 p.m. on Monday, November 2, 2026.
From the Alabama Secretary of State .
What is a provisional ballot in Alabama, and does it count?
If your name isn't on the voter list and the county can't confirm your registration, you can vote a provisional ballot. It counts if your county Board of Registrars confirms you're qualified to vote at that polling place.
