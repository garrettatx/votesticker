I run VoteSticker.org, a free nonpartisan site about "I Voted" stickers, so I have a conflict of interest and am not editing the article directly. I'm suggesting two changes for an uninvolved editor to consider.

'''1. Failed citation.''' The History section says "A 1950 article from the Miami Herald mentions an 'I Have Voted' sticker," cited to Nelson, "'I Voted' stickers, explained" (Vox, November 8, 2016). That Vox article doesn't mention 1950 or the Miami Herald; its earliest example is Miami businesses offering discounts to sticker wearers in 1982. It also doesn't mention the 26th Amendment or 1971, which the same sentence attributes to it. https://www.vox.com/presidential-election/2016/11/8/13553112/i-voted-sticker A source that does give details is Jake Rossen, "A Brief but Fascinating History of the 'I Voted' Sticker," Mental Floss (updated October 29, 2024): it dates the item to the October 27, 1950 Miami Herald and says "I Have Voted" stickers were to be handed out at the polls "as a reminder to others to cast their vote." https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/government-politics/i-voted-sticker-history

'''2. An earlier predecessor.''' John Palmer Gavit's ''Americans by Choice'' (Harper & Brothers, 1922), pp. 330–333, describes a Grand Rapids, Michigan campaign in which committees of women gave voters paper tags at the polls at the March and April 1919 city elections. Gavit writes that "the only way to get a tag was to vote" and that the tag read "I am an American. I voted. Did you?" Schoolchildren then collected the tags for prizes. Full text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60576/60576-h/60576-h.htm (chapter X). This predates the 1920 button the article currently describes as the earliest predecessor.
