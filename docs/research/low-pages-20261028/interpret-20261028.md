# Perplexity Read: Interpreting the Clearscope Reports and Search Console (October 28, 2026)

Thread: https://www.perplexity.ai/search/585b52a8-ee4d-4185-a7e3-769439328773 (53 sources; prompt in interpret-prompt.txt).

## Main Points
- Sticker first, not longer guides or higher grades. Home: get a free digital sticker fast. State pages: the
  state-themed sticker plus the official designs people search for; voting help close but second.
- Intent by query: plain "i voted sticker" is mixed (buy, digital image, learn, see designs), so be clear we
  offer free digital stickers and a maker. "[state] i voted sticker" wants our state sticker and the official
  designs, contests, artists and where to get one (strongest in MI and KS). "i voted early sticker" wants an
  early design. "how to get / by mail" is an access problem: answer the paper-sticker question from local
  official sources, then offer the digital one.
- Localization: Dallas vs national looks like a national core with a regional layer. Google says location,
  language and device affect results even without personalization (support.google). Our snapshots can't
  separate the effect of the state name from the searcher's location, and GSC gives landing page, not the
  searcher's state.
- Weighting: voter usefulness and accuracy, then Search Console, then a look at real results and the official
  pages behind them, then Clearscope as a topic-gap checklist. Google has no preferred word count. Names,
  mixed intent and geography inflate "missing terms". Keep 7th grade.
- Michigan order: brief intro and small sticker preview, the official 2026 designs, where to get them, our
  gallery and maker, early/mail sticker questions, compact voting help. Kansas: add Johnson, Wyandotte,
  Montgomery next to Sedgwick, and separate "won a contest" from "available in this county in 2026".

## Students and Artwork
First names only is a sound privacy choice, not a legal safe harbor: report only what the election office
published, use the least identifying detail, offer a correction contact. Official artwork is not public domain
by default (17 U.S.C. 105 covers federal works only); describe and link official designs rather than
redistributing them unless permission is clear. Suggested disclosure: our stickers aren't proof of voting, and
official designs belong to their creators.
