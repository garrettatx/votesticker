Vote Early
Early voting in Minnesota runs Friday, September 18 to Monday, November 2. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
Vote at your county or city election office; the last day is Monday, November 2, until 5 p.m.
Election Day hours: Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. Some small towns open later, but no later than 10 a.m.

Where You Can Vote
Vote early in person starting Friday, September 18. From Friday, October 16, early voting works like Election Day: you put your ballot straight into a ballot counter. Every county has at least one early voting location.
Enter your address in the Polling Place Finder to see where you vote on Election Day. Early voting locations are listed by county on the Secretary of State's site.
Not registered? Register at your polling place on Election Day. Bring a Minnesota driver's license or ID with your current address, or a photo ID plus a bill or lease with your current address. A registered voter from your precinct can also go with you and vouch for your address.

What ID to Bring
Registering at the polls with a photo ID and a bill or lease? The photo ID can be expired.
If your registration is current and you haven't moved or changed your name, you don't need ID. If you need to register or update it, or haven't voted in four years or more, bring proof of where you live.

Who Can Vote by Mail
To get a ballot by mail, apply for one online or on paper. You don't need to be registered first.
Find a witness before you vote your ballot: any U.S. citizen who will be 18 by Election Day, or a notary. Your witness needs to see your blank ballot first, and you both fill out the white signature envelope.
Some counties and cities have secure drop boxes you can use any time. On Election Day, get your ballot to a drop box or the elections office by 5 p.m.
If your ballot is rejected more than five days before the election, you'll get a replacement ballot in the mail with the reason. Closer to Election Day, election officials will try to contact you.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Cover up or take off campaign shirts and buttons inside the polling place. Minnesota bans wearing anything about a candidate, party or ballot question on that day's ballot there, and no one can campaign within 100 feet of the building.
Save the sticker selfie for outside. No Minnesota law strictly bans photos in the polling place, but the Secretary of State discourages them, and showing your marked ballot to others is against the law.
Get in line by 8 p.m. on Election Day and you can vote, even if you reach the front after 8.

Minnesota and the I Voted Sticker
Minnesota's I voted sticker is written into state law: it says "I VOTED," and nothing more. Election officials can give one to you when you put your ballot in the ballot box, and to people who vote by mail too.

Questions People Ask

When are polls open in Minnesota on Election Day?
Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. Some small towns open later, but no later than 10 a.m.
From Minnesota Statutes .
Do I need ID to vote in Minnesota?
No, if you're already registered; you sign the polling place roster. If you register at the polls, you'll need proof of where you live.
From Minnesota Statutes .
Can I still register to vote in Minnesota?
Yes. Preregistration closed October 13, 2026, but you can register at your polling place on Election Day. Bring proof of where you live.
From Minnesota Statutes .
Can I take a picture of my ballot in Minnesota?
Not at the polls. If you show your marked ballot to anyone at the polling place, election judges set it aside as spoiled.
From Minnesota Statutes .
When is the mail ballot deadline in Minnesota?
Election officials must receive your mailed ballot by Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, and a postmark doesn't count. Dropping it off yourself? Get it to the office that sent it by 5 p.m. on Election Day, not to your polling place.
From the Minnesota Secretary of State .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Minnesota?
You can still vote. If you're in line by 8 p.m., you get to vote, even if you reach the front after 8.
From the Minnesota Secretary of State .
How do I track my mail ballot in Minnesota?
Use the state's Track your ballot tool to see when your ballot was mailed to you, when it came back and whether it was accepted.
From the Minnesota Secretary of State .
How do I make it my profile picture?
Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
