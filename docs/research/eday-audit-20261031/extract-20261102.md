- Lookup: https://www.elections.virginia.gov/casting-a-ballot/polling-place-lookup/
- Polls FAQ: Polling places are open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. If you're in line at 7 p.m., you can vote. From the Virginia Department of Elections .
- Line: You can still vote. Anyone in line at 7 p.m. on Election Day will be allowed to vote. From the Virginia Department of Elections .
- Register: Yes, in person. The regular deadline was October 23, 2026. During early voting, register at your general registrar's office or a satellite site; on Election Day, go to the polling place for the precinct where you live. You vote a provisional ballot. From the Virginia Department of Elections .
- ID: Show an acceptable ID, which doesn't need a photo. If you don't have one, you can sign an ID Confirmation Statement and vote a regular ballot. From the Virginia Department of Elections .
- Late mail: Not if it's postmarked by Tuesday, November 3, and reaches your general registrar by noon on Friday, November 6. Mailing it this late is a risk. On Election Day, you can drop it off at a polling place in your locality or at your general registrar's office by 7 p.m. From Virginia law .

## washington
- Banner: Still have your ballot? Check how to return it. | Drop box: by 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3. Mail: postmarked on or before November 3, and no stamp needed. Postmarks aren't guaranteed on the day you mail, so use a drop box now.
- Lookup: https://voter.votewa.gov/WhereToVote.aspx
- Polls FAQ: 
- Line: You can still vote. Anyone in line at a voting center or ballot drop box at 8 p.m. can vote and drop off their ballot. From the Washington Secretary of State .
- Register: Yes, in person. Online and mail registrations had to arrive by October 26, 2026. You can still register in person until 8 p.m. on Election Day. From the Washington Secretary of State .
- ID: Not to vote by mail. Your county checks the signature on your ballot envelope. If you vote in person at a voting center, you sign a ballot declaration or show a photo ID, such as a driver's license, state ID, student ID or tribal ID. From Washington law .
- Late mail: A mailed ballot still counts if it's postmarked by November 3, but a postmark isn't guaranteed the day you mail it. This close, the Secretary of State says to use a drop box instead. A ballot in an official drop box by 8 p.m. on November 3 is on time, and you can also hand it in at a county elections office or voting center. Find locations at VoteWA.gov. From the Washington Secretary of State .

## west-virginia
- Banner: Find your Election Day voting location and hours. | Scheduled voting hours: Polling places are open 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. local time.
- Lookup: https://apps.sos.wv.gov/Elections/Voter/FindMyPollingPlace
- Polls FAQ: Polls are open 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. at your assigned polling place. From the West Virginia Secretary of State .
- Line: You can still vote. Anyone in line at the polling place when the polls close gets to vote, even if the line runs outside. From the West Virginia Code .
- Register: No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 13, 2026, and West Virginia has no Election Day registration. From the West Virginia Secretary of State .
- ID: Yes. Show an unexpired photo ID, such as a West Virginia driver's license, a U.S. passport, or a West Virginia student ID. Without one, an adult or poll worker who has known you for six months can vouch for you; otherwise you vote a provisional ballot. From the West Virginia Secretary of State .
- Late mail: A mailed absentee ballot counts if your county clerk receives it by Wednesday, November 4. A later one counts if it's postmarked by November 3 and arrives before canvass starts on Monday, November 9. From the West Virginia Secretary of State .

## wisconsin
- Banner: Find your Election Day voting location and hours. | Scheduled voting hours: Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. local time.
- Lookup: https://myvote.wi.gov/en-us/
- Polls FAQ: Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. Absentee ballots must reach your municipal clerk by 8 p.m. on Election Day. From MyVote Wisconsin .
- Line: You can still vote. Anyone waiting in line when polls close at 8 p.m. is allowed to vote. From Wisconsin law .
- Register: Yes, at your polling place on Election Day. Online and mail registration closed October 14, 2026, and registration at your clerk's office ended at 5 p.m. on October 30. Bring proof of where you live. From MyVote Wisconsin .
- ID: Yes. Show an acceptable photo ID, such as a Wisconsin driver's license, a free state ID card, or a U.S. passport. Without one, you vote a provisional ballot and must show ID afterward. From the Wisconsin Elections Commission .
- Late mail: It may be. Your ballot must reach election officials by 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, and the Elections Commission recommends mailing it at least 10 days ahead. Instead, drop it off yourself at your municipal clerk's office, or on Election Day at your polling place or absentee counting location. From the Wisconsin Elections Commission .

## wyoming
- Banner: Find your Election Day voting location and hours. | Scheduled voting hours: Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time.
- Lookup: https://myelectionday.sos.wyo.gov/WYVOTES/Pages/VOSearch.aspx
- Polls FAQ: Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. If you're in line at 7 p.m., you can still vote. From the Wyoming Secretary of State .
- Line: You can still vote. Polls stay open until the last person in line at 7 p.m. has voted. From the Wyoming Secretary of State .
- Register: Yes. Regular registration closed October 19, 2026, but you can register and vote at the same time during early absentee voting or on Election Day. Bring proof of identity, citizenship, and residence. From the Wyoming Secretary of State .
- ID: Yes. Show an acceptable ID, such as a Wyoming driver's license or ID card, a U.S. passport, a tribal ID, or a Medicare or Medicaid card. No acceptable ID with you? You can still vote a provisional ballot after signing an oath. It counts only if you bring proof to your county clerk by close of business Wednesday, November 4. From the Wyoming Election Code .
- Late mail: It counts only if your county clerk receives it by 7 p.m. on Election Day, whatever the postmark. If time is short, bring it to the clerk's office yourself. From the Wyoming Secretary of State .
