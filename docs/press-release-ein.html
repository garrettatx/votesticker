VoteSticker.org Finds Record of 'I Have Voted' Tags at a St. Louis School in 1920

Nonpartisan site offers a sourced history of the 'I voted' sticker and free digital stickers for every state

VoteSticker.org, a free, nonpartisan site built by Austin SEO consultancy Garrett Digital, has found a record of paper "I have voted" tags from October 1920. According to a St. Louis Board of Education report, Blewett, a St. Louis public school, made the tags in its print shop for a student election and gave one to each student who voted. These tags are the earliest example the site has found.

VoteSticker.org offers free digital "I voted" stickers for every state and Washington, D.C. Visitors pick a state, save a ready-made design or make their own, and share it as a profile picture or in a post, so friends see they voted. The site's history page puts the sticker's story in one place, with a source for each entry.

Other entries include a 1962 Camp Fire Girls handbook that suggested handing out tags on Election Day; a 1968 U.S. Commission on Civil Rights report describing tags pinned on voters leaving a Georgia polling place in 1966; a 1985 Phoenix Board of Realtors campaign; and the 1987 waving-flag oval designed by Janet Boudreau of Intab. The page also lists what is still unknown, including who printed the first adhesive "I voted" sticker.

"I built VoteSticker.org because getting an 'I voted' sticker is a small thing people enjoy, and sharing one online is an easy way to remind friends to vote," said Garrett Nafzinger, founder of Garrett Digital and creator of VoteSticker.org. "I wanted to make it easy to find one you like and share it. And if someone still needs voting information, the site points them to official sources."

The stickers come in English and Spanish, with "I voted," "I voted by mail" and first-time voter designs. No account or email address is required, and nothing is for sale. Each fits a circular profile picture, and custom stickers are made in the visitor's browser, not uploaded to the site.

State pages also cover official sticker designs and contest winners, many from 2026, and link to election offices and official ballot trackers where a state has one. VoteSticker.org is not a government website, and its stickers are not official election-office designs or proof of voting.
