Where You Can Vote
Bring ID whether you vote early or on Election Day. You need it to get a ballot at an in-person early voting location too. On Election Day, polls are open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m.
Your county runs its own voting locations. In Maricopa County, look yours up at locations.maricopa.vote. In Pima County, use pima.gov. Anywhere else, check with your county election office.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
One photo ID with your name and address: an Arizona driver license, an Arizona non-operating ID card, a tribal ID, or another valid U.S. federal, state or local government ID
Or two documents with your name and address, such as a utility bill or a bank or credit union statement dated within 90 days of the election, Arizona vehicle registration, an Arizona vehicle insurance card, a property tax statement, your voter registration card, or mail marked "Official Election Material"
Or a photo ID whose address doesn't match, a U.S. passport or a U.S. military ID, plus one document from the list above
No acceptable ID? You vote a conditional provisional ballot. To make it count, show ID to your county recorder by 5 p.m. on the 5th business day after the election.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered voter can get a ballot by mail. Join the Active Early Voting List to get one for every election, or ask for one for this election only, online, by phone, by email or with a written form to your county recorder.
Sign the affidavit on the return envelope. Your county compares that signature with the one on your registration record.
New in 2026: when you drop off your sealed ballot at a voting location, you can show your ID there. The envelope is stamped "ID verified" and your ballot is ready to count without the later signature check.
If your signature looks different from the one on file, your county will try to reach you so you can fix it, until the 5th business day after the election. If you forgot to sign, they'll contact you, and you have until 7 p.m. on Election Day to add it.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Wear what you like. Arizona lets voters wear clothing with a political message inside the 75-foot limit. Campaigning there is out, though: no handing out literature or talking to voters about candidates or issues.
Take your sticker selfie outside the 75-foot line. Photos and video are not allowed within 75 feet of a voting location.
Polls close at 7 p.m. If you're in line when they close, stay put. You'll be allowed to vote.

Questions People Ask
Answers come from official Arizona sources. Last checked October 26, 2026.
When are polls open in Arizona on Election Day?
Polls are open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
Do I need ID to vote in Arizona?
Yes. Show one photo ID with your name and address, such as an Arizona driver license or a tribal ID. Without one, two documents such as a utility bill and a bank statement can work.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
Can I still register to vote in Arizona?
No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 5, 2026, and Arizona has no Election Day registration.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
Can I take a picture of my ballot in Arizona?
Yes, outside the polling place. Arizona allows posting a photo of your own ballot, but no one may take photos or video within 75 feet of a polling place.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
When is the mail ballot deadline in Arizona?
Drop it off at any polling place or voting location in your county by 7 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3. By mail, your county recorder must receive it by 7 p.m. that day, so it's too late to count on the mail now.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
What is a provisional ballot in Arizona, and does it count?
You may get one if you were sent an early ballot, your name isn't on the roster, you're on inactive status, or you changed your name or moved within the county without updating your registration. It counts once the county confirms you're registered and eligible. In counties without vote centers, you also need to be in the right precinct.
From the Arizona Secretary of State .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Arizona?
You can still vote. Anyone qualified who is in line when the polls close at 7 p.m. is allowed to vote.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
How do I track my mail ballot in Arizona?
Use Track My Ballot at trackmyballot.azsos.gov. It shows whether your county got your ballot and whether it was verified and sent to be counted, or rejected.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in Arizona?
Your county will try to reach you. Add your signature by 7 p.m. on Election Day or your ballot won't count.
From the Arizona Revised Statutes .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in Arizona?
Arizona counts mailed ballots only if they arrive by 7 p.m. on Election Day. After Tuesday, October 27, skip the mail and drop it off at any polling place or voting location in your county.
From the Arizona Citizens Clean Elections Commission .

Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
