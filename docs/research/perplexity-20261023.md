# Perplexity Research, October 23, 2026

Garrett's Perplexity runs on the topics from `docs/search-console-priorities-20261023.html`. This is a digest
with the sources each finding rests on. Nothing here is verified by us yet: before any of it goes on a
page, open the source, confirm it says what is claimed and that it applies to 2026, and record it in the
state's facts file the way the FAQs were done (`docs/faq-sources.json`, `qa_facts.py`).

The rule the research keeps arriving at: **stickers are a county decision, not a state one.** A county's
practice never becomes a statement about the whole state, and a design announcement is not proof of
where to get one.

## 1. Do Early Voters Get a Sticker?

Not consistently. Depending on the office, early voters get a standard "I voted," an early-voting
design, a sticker in the mail-ballot packet, or a digital version. EAC documents programs run by both
state and local offices ([EAC sticker contest best practices](https://www.eac.gov/blogs/best-practices-i-voted-sticker-contests)).

## 6. What This Suggests for VoteSticker (For the Content Plan)

1. Keep the message a voter's own choice: before (Vote early, I'm voting early, Vote Nov 3) and after
   (I voted, I voted early, I voted by mail). The maker already works this way.
2. FAQ on every state page: "Do I get a sticker if I vote early?" and "I voted by mail. Where do I get a
   sticker?" National answer first, then the state's named counties where we have a source.
3. A "Where to get a physical sticker" block for the mail-heavy states (WA, OR, UT, CO, CA, NV, HI, DC),
   with named counties only.
4. A gallery of each state's official 2026 design and its story, which is what the outranking roundups
   have and our pages don't. Images need rights: link out or describe unless the office allows reuse.
