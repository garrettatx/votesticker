When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote on Election Day
New Hampshire doesn’t have early voting open to everyone. Most voters vote on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot.
Election Day hours: Hours vary by town and city. Polls open by 11 a.m. and close no earlier than 7 p.m., and many open earlier. Your town or city clerk has the exact hours.
ID: Yes. Show a valid photo ID, such as a driver's license from any state, a state ID card, a U.S. military ID, or a U.S. passport. Without one, an election official must confirm who you are before you can vote.

Where You Can Vote
Vote at your town or city polling place on Election Day. If you'll be away, can't get there during polling hours because of work or caring for someone, have a disability, or can't appear in public because of a religious commitment, you can vote absentee instead.
From New Hampshire law . Last checked October 26, 2026.
What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Driver's license from any state or the federal government
New Hampshire ID card, or a non-driver ID card from another state
U.S. armed services ID card
U.S. passport or passport card
Your ID can be expired up to five years. If you're 65 or older, the expiration date doesn't matter.
No photo ID? You'll see the supervisor of the checklist, who decides whether your identity can be verified. A moderator, clerk, supervisor or selectman who knows you personally can vouch for you. If your identity isn't established, you can't vote, but you can appeal right away to the superior court.

Who Can Vote by Mail
You can vote absentee in New Hampshire if you:
will be away from your town or city on Election Day
can't get to your polling place at any time during polling hours because of work, including caring for children or infirm adults
can't appear in public on Election Day because of a religious commitment
can't vote in person because of a physical disability
Seal your ballot in the inner envelope and sign the affidavit on it. If someone helps you because you're blind or have a disability, that person signs a statement on the envelope too.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Save the campaigning for outside. Electioneering isn't allowed inside the polling place building, and the moderator sets up a no-electioneering corridor at least 10 feet wide from each entrance, where handing out or posting signs, pins, stickers or campaign clothing is banned.
If you're in line when the polls close, including the line to register, stay in it. You can still register and vote.

New Hampshire and the I Voted Sticker
New Hampshire's Secretary of State ran an I Voted sticker contest for fourth and fifth graders, with the theme "Celebrating 250 Years of New Hampshire History!" A panel of local election officials picks the winning designs, which are handed out as stickers to voters in the 2026 state elections.

When are polls open in New Hampshire on Election Day?
Hours vary by town and city. Polls open by 11 a.m. and close no earlier than 7 p.m., and many open earlier. Your town or city clerk has the exact hours.
From New Hampshire law .
Do I need ID to vote in New Hampshire?
Yes. Show a valid photo ID, such as a driver's license from any state, a state ID card, a U.S. military ID, or a U.S. passport. Without one, an election official must confirm who you are before you can vote.
From New Hampshire law .
Can I still register to vote in New Hampshire?
Yes. You can register with your town or city clerk before the last checklist session in late October, or at your polling place on Election Day. You'll need proof of U.S. citizenship.
From New Hampshire law .
When is the mail ballot deadline in New Hampshire?
Your town or city clerk must receive it by 5 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3. Mail it with a stamp, bring it in yourself, or have a close family member deliver it with a signed form and photo ID. A postmark isn't enough.
From New Hampshire law .
What if I am in line when the polls close in New Hampshire?
You can still vote. Everyone in line when the polls close, including the line to register, gets to register if needed and vote.

How do I make it my profile picture?

When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
