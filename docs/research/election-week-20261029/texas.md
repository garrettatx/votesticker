## Election-week voter facts

All sources below are official **.gov** pages. Quoted wording is preserved; explanatory headings distinguish what the quote establishes.

### Missing name and registration

**1. A missing name does not automatically mean a provisional ballot.** A valid registration certificate and acceptable photo ID may permit a regular ballot. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “As another example, if your name does not appear on the official list of registered voters in the precinct, you may be able to cast a regular ballot by presenting your valid voter registration certificate along with an acceptable form of photo identification.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**2. If registration cannot be resolved, ask for a provisional ballot rather than simply leaving.** The state lists this among registered voters’ rights. [Official voter-rights page](https://www.votetexas.gov/your-rights/index.html)

> “Cast a provisional ballot if your name does not appear on the list of registered voters.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/your-rights/index.html](https://www.votetexas.gov/your-rights/index.html)

**3. Mention a registration or address update made through DPS.** The state’s election-worker instructions specifically require asking about that transaction when a voter believes they are registered. [sos.texas](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/forms/special-forms-of-voting.pdf)

> “If the voter believes they are registered, the election judge must ask if the voter registered, or updated their registration, at DPS.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/forms/special-forms-of-voting.pdf](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/forms/special-forms-of-voting.pdf)

**4. “Suspense” is not an automatic disqualification.** The FAQ describes a same-county voter’s Election Day option. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “A voter on Suspense who has moved within the same county would be allowed to vote on Election Day by returning to the precinct in which the voter previously resided and completing a Statement of Residence at that time.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**5. A limited ballot is not an Election Day fallback for moving between counties.** That option is confined to early voting. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “This procedure is only available during the early voting period at the main early voting polling place; you may NOT vote a limited ballot on election day.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

### Provisional ballots and help

**6. How to check a provisional ballot: request its disposition from the county election office, and watch for the mailed outcome.** Texas law requires free access to disposition information; the state FAQ specifies when the mailed notice is due. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/) [statutes.capitol.texas](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/EL/htm/EL.65.htm)

> “The secretary of state shall prescribe procedures to implement a system to allow a person who casts a provisional ballot under Section 63.011 to obtain access free of charge to information on the disposition of the person's ballot.”

**Official URL:** [https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/EL/htm/EL.65.htm](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/EL/htm/EL.65.htm) — Section 65.059

> “Provisional voters will receive a notice in the mail by the 10th day after the local canvass advising them if their provisional ballots were counted, and if they were not counted, the reason why.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**Important distinction:** I did not verify a statewide online provisional-ballot lookup. Do not present the ballot-by-mail tracker as a provisional-ballot tracker.

**7. An ID-related provisional ballot may require action by Monday, November 9.** This cure requirement concerns the identification problem—not every reason for voting provisionally. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/) [2026 calendar](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml)

> “In order to have the provisional ballot counted in the election, you will have to visit the county voter registrar’s office by the sixth calendar day after Election Day.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**8. The state voter-help number is 1-800-252-VOTE (8683).** The state’s election-specific FAQ supplies it directly. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “Should you need additional information, please e-mail or call us at 1-800-252-VOTE(8683).”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**9. Your ID address does not have to match the voter-registration address.** This answers the FAQ’s question about whether an ID is acceptable—not whether the voter is registered in the correct jurisdiction. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “There is no address matching requirement.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

### Last-minute voting options

**10. November 1 and 2 are not regular early-voting days.** Early voting ends the preceding Friday. [Official FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

> “Early voting by personal appearance for the November 3, 2026 Election begins on October 19, 2026 and ends on October 30, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/)

**11. A voter may personally hand-deliver their marked mail ballot to the early voting clerk on Election Day.** The calendar specifies the clerk’s office and requires identification; this is not a general authorization for another person to drop it off. [Official 2026 calendar](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml)

> “A voter may deliver a marked ballot by mail to the early voting clerk’s office while the polls are open on election day.”

> “The voter must provide an acceptable form of identification under Section 63.0101 upon delivery.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml) — November 3 entry

An emergency sickness/disability ballot has a separate Election Day application deadline.** The calendar places this entry under **5:00 p.m. on November 3**; the completed ballot’s receipt deadline is **7:00 p.m. that day**. [Official 2026 calendar](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml)

> “Deadline for receiving Application for Emergency Early Voting Ballot Due to Sickness or Physical Disability (PDF) for late ballots to be voted by persons who became sick or disabled on or after Thursday, October 22, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml) — November 3, 5:00 p.m. entry

### Mail ballots after Tuesday

**13. Some domestic mail ballots can arrive Wednesday, November 4—but the postmark requirement matters.** This provision concerns voters using the regular Application for Ballot by Mail, not the Federal Post Card Application. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “However, you may count a “late domestic ballot” from a voter using an Application for Ballot by Mail (“ABBM”) (not a Federal Post Card Application (“FPCA”)) that arrives no later than 5:00 p.m. on the next business day after election day (Wednesday, November 4, 2026) if it bears a cancellation mark of the postal service or a receipt mark of a common or contract carrier or a courier not later than 7:00 p.m. on November 3, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

**14. Certain overseas and military ballots have a November 9 receipt deadline.** The application type and voter category determine the rule. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “The last day to receive a ballot from an overseas non-military voter using an FPCA is Monday, November 9, 2026.”

> “The last day to receive a ballot from a military voter using an FPCA is the sixth day after the election, which is Monday, November 9, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

**15. Some mail-envelope defects can still be corrected after Election Day.** The in-person deadline is November 9. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “The last day a voter may come in person to the early voting clerk’s office to correct certain defects on their carrier envelope is also the sixth day after election day, which is Monday, November 9, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

**16. A legally timely ballot arriving after Tuesday is not necessarily counted Wednesday.** The ballot board has until November 16 to complete the specified late-ballot, provisional-ballot, and corrected-envelope work. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “The last day for the early voting ballot board to convene and count “late domestic ballots,” provisional ballots, overseas ballots, and ballots contained in previously defective carrier envelopes which were corrected by voters by the required deadline is the 13th day after the election, which is Monday, November 16, 2026.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

### Canvass and certification timing

**17. The local canvass window is November 6–17, not Election Night.** A canvass is the official review and aggregation of election returns; the advisory also requires completion of the applicable provisional, late-arriving, and corrected-envelope counts before it occurs. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “The local canvass for the November 3, 2026 uniform election must be held not earlier than the third day after election day (Friday, November 6, 2026) and not later than the 14th day after election day (Tuesday, November 17, 2026).”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

**18. There is not one statewide finalization date for every office.** County and precinct contests end at the local canvass; district, federal, and statewide contests proceed to the state canvass. [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “For all county and precinct races, the final canvass is the local canvass.”

> “For all district (including single-county districts), federal, and statewide races, the state canvass is the final canvass.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

**19. The state canvass window is November 21–December 7; the actual date was not yet set in the October 23 advisory.** Describe this as a window, not a scheduled “certification day.” [Official post-election advisory](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)

> “The state canvass for the November 3, 2026 General Election must be held no earlier than the 18th day after election day (Saturday, November 21, 2026) and no later than the 33rd day after election day (Monday, December 7, 2026).”

> “The final state canvass date for the general election has not been set at press time, so those deadlines cannot yet be determined.”

**Official URL:** [https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-28.shtml)
