When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote Early
Early voting in Virginia runs Friday, September 18 to Saturday, October 31. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
Every locality offers early voting. Vote in the locality where you are registered.
Election Day hours: Polling places are open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. If you're in line at 7 p.m., you can vote.
ID: Show an acceptable ID, which doesn't need a photo. If you don't have one, you can sign an ID Confirmation Statement and vote a regular ballot.

Where You Can Vote
During early voting, go to your general registrar's office or any early voting site in your county or city. You don't need a reason. On Election Day, vote at your assigned polling place.
Enter your address in the state's lookup tool to see your Election Day polling place, early voting dates, hours and locations, ballot drop-off options and what's on your ballot.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Virginia driver's license, current or expired
Virginia DMV-issued ID card, current or expired
Employee photo ID from your employer, public or private
U.S. military ID
Student ID from a Virginia high school, college or university
Student photo ID from a U.S. college or university
U.S. passport or passport card
Government-issued ID from the federal government, Virginia or a local government
Voter ID card or voter confirmation documents from the Department of Elections
Tribal ID from one of the 11 tribes Virginia recognizes
Nursing home resident ID from a government facility
Utility bill, bank statement, government check or paycheck with your name and address, no more than 12 months old
Any other current government document with your name and address
A Virginia driver's license or DMV ID card counts even if it's expired. Other IDs can't be expired more than 12 months.
Forgot your ID? Sign an ID Confirmation Statement and vote a regular ballot. If you don't sign it, you vote a provisional ballot and have until noon on the Friday after the election to get a copy of your ID or a signed statement to your local electoral board.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered voter in Virginia can vote absentee by mail. You don't need a reason.
Fill in and sign the statement on the back of the ballot envelope. Leaving out your middle name or the date won't void your ballot.
Drop-off locations are set by each locality. The instructions in your ballot mailing list the ones near you.
Made a mistake on the envelope? If your ballot reaches the registrar by the Friday before Election Day, the registrar will tell you by mail or email within three days, and you can fix it until noon on the third day after the election.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
You can wear your campaign shirt, hat, button or sticker when you go in to vote. What's not allowed within 40 feet of a polling place entrance: hanging around, handing out campaign material or trying to sway anyone's vote.
You can use your phone inside the polling place. Election officers can stop phone use that slows down or disrupts voting, or that tries to sway another voter.
In line at your polling place at 7 p.m.? Stay in line. You can still vote; people who arrive later can't.

When is the mail ballot deadline in Virginia?
Bring it to your general registrar's office by 7 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, or use a drop-off location in your locality. By mail, it must be postmarked on or before November 3 and reach your registrar by noon on the third day after the election.

What is a provisional ballot in Virginia, and does it count?
If you come without an acceptable ID and don't sign an ID Confirmation Statement, you vote a provisional ballot. It counts if your ID copy or signed statement reaches your local electoral board by noon on the Friday after the election. A Friday postmark isn't enough.
From the Virginia Department of Elections .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Virginia?
You can still vote. Anyone in line at 7 p.m. on Election Day will be allowed to vote.
