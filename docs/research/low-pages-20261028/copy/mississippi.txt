Where You Can Vote
Mississippi has no early voting for everyone. If you qualify for absentee voting, for example you're 65 or older, will be away from home on Election Day, or have to work while the polls are open, you can vote absentee in person at your county's circuit clerk's office. Bring your photo ID and be ready to give your reason.
Enter your home address in My Election Day to find your polling place and sample ballot. Polling places are updated by local officials, so it's a good idea to confirm yours with your county circuit clerk.
First time voting after registering by mail? If your registration wasn't verified, you'll have to show ID the first time you go to the polls.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Mississippi driver's license, including the Mississippi Mobile ID
ID card from any Mississippi state agency
U.S. passport
Employee photo ID from the U.S. government, the State of Mississippi, or a Mississippi county, city or other local government entity
Mississippi license to carry a pistol or revolver, with your photo
Tribal photo ID
U.S. military photo ID
Student photo ID from an accredited Mississippi college, university, or community or junior college
Mississippi Voter Identification Card
Any other photo ID from the U.S. government or another state government
Most IDs on the list count as valid if they have no expiration date or were issued in the last 10 years. The carry license, the student ID and other government photo IDs must not be expired.
No photo ID on Election Day? You'll vote an affidavit ballot. To make it count, show an acceptable photo ID at your circuit clerk's office by 5 p.m. on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. No ID at all? The circuit clerk's office issues a free Mississippi Voter ID Card.

Who Can Vote by Mail
You can vote absentee by mail in Mississippi if you:
are temporarily living outside your county of residence (the ballot must be mailed to an address outside your city or town)
have a temporary or permanent physical disability that makes voting in person a substantial hardship or could put you or others in danger
are the parent, spouse or dependent of a person with a disability who is hospitalized outside your county or more than 50 miles from home, and will be with them on Election Day
are 65 or older
are incarcerated but have not been convicted of a disenfranchising crime
Mark your ballot in front of a notary or another official who can give oaths, then sign the affidavit on the back of the envelope and have them sign too. If you have a temporary or permanent physical disability, any witness 18 or older can sign instead. The application needs the same kind of witness.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign shirts and buttons at home. It's against the law to post or hand out campaign literature within 150 feet of the polling place, unless it's on private property, and that includes T-shirts, buttons and the like with a candidate's name or likeness. Loitering within 30 feet is out too.

When are polls open in Mississippi on Election Day?
Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.

Do I need ID to vote in Mississippi?

Can I still register to vote in Mississippi?
No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 5, 2026, and Mississippi has no Election Day registration.

When is the mail ballot deadline in Mississippi?
Mail your ballot to your circuit clerk postmarked on or before Election Day, Tuesday, November 3. The clerk must receive it by 5 p.m. on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, for it to count.
From the Mississippi Secretary of State .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Mississippi?
You can still vote. If you're in line at 7 p.m., you have the right to cast your ballot.

How do I make it my profile picture?

When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
