Where You Can Vote
Nevada mails you a ballot, but you can still vote in person during early voting or on Election Day. Your county may set up vote centers where anyone registered in the county can vote on Election Day. If you already have a mail ballot, bring it to surrender, or sign a statement that you haven't voted.
The Secretary of State's map shows every early voting site, Election Day polling place and official drop box in the state. Each county also posts its own list.
Registered online in the last two weeks? You'll vote in person, at an early voting site or an Election Day polling place. Tell the poll worker you registered online before you came.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
A current and valid photo ID that shows your physical address
A current utility bill
A bank statement
A paycheck
A government document or check with your name and address (a voter registration card doesn't count)
Most voters just sign. ID is for some first-time voters who registered by mail or online and haven't voted for a federal office in Nevada before. A photo ID has to be current and valid.
If you're asked for ID and don't have it, you can vote a provisional ballot. It counts if you bring the ID to your county clerk by 5 p.m. on Friday, November 6, 2026.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Every registered voter gets a ballot in the mail. Rather vote in person only? Send your county clerk the Mail Ballot Preference Form to opt out.
If you have a disability, are 65 or older, or can't read or write, someone can help you mark and sign your ballot. They note next to the signature that they signed for you, or put their name, address and signature on the return envelope if they helped.
Every polling place, including early voting sites, has a ballot drop box. You can drop your ballot at any official drop box in your county until polls close on Election Day.
If you didn't sign, or your signature doesn't match, your county clerk will contact you by mail, by phone if they have your number, and by email if you gave it. Respond by 5 p.m. on Monday, November 9.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign buttons and badges at home. Wearing a badge, button or other item that names a party, candidate or ballot question counts as electioneering, which isn't allowed inside a polling place or within 100 feet of the building's entrance.
Take your sticker selfie outside. Nevada law bars the public from photographing or recording voting at a polling place.
In line when polls close at 7 p.m.? Stay in line. You have the right to vote, during early voting and on Election Day.

When are polls open in Nevada on Election Day?
Polling places are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Nevada also mails a ballot to every active voter. Mail it postmarked by Election Day so it arrives by 5 p.m. on November 7, or drop it off before polls close.

Do I need ID to vote in Nevada?
Usually not; most voters sign to vote. Some first-time voters who registered by mail or online may need a photo ID, a utility bill, or a bank statement.

Can I still register to vote in Nevada?
Yes. You can register online through Election Day, or register and vote in person during early voting or on Election Day. Mail registration closed October 6, 2026.

Can I take a picture of my ballot in Nevada?
Not at the polls. Nevada law bars the public from photographing voting at a polling place.

When is the mail ballot deadline in Nevada?
Drop it at a drop box or your county clerk by 7 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, when polls close. By mail: postmarked on or before November 3 and received by 5 p.m. on Saturday, November 7, 2026. The Secretary of State recommends mailing it at least a week before Election Day.
From Nevada election law (NRS chapter 293) .
What is a provisional ballot in Nevada, and does it count?
It's a ballot you vote when something can't be confirmed at the polls. It counts if your county clerk confirms you're registered and eligible. If it was for missing ID, bring the ID to the clerk by 5 p.m. on Friday, November 6.
From Nevada election law (NRS chapter 293) .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Nevada?
Stay in line. If you're waiting in line when polls close, you have the right to vote.

What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in Nevada?
Your county clerk will contact you by mail, and by phone or email if they have them, so you can sign. Do it by 5 p.m. on Monday, November 9, or your ballot won't count.
From Nevada election law (NRS chapter 293) .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in Nevada?
A mailed ballot counts if it's postmarked on or before November 3 and reaches your county clerk by 5 p.m. on November 7. The Secretary of State recommends mailing it at least a week ahead, so in the last week, use a drop box.
