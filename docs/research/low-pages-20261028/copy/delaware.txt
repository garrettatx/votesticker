Where You Can Vote
During early voting, vote at any early voting site in your county. On Election Day, vote at your assigned polling place from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m.
Look yourself up on iVote to find your Election Day polling place. It's also on your Polling Place Card. Early voting sites for each county are on the Department's Voting Locations page.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Delaware driver's license
Delaware state ID card
A copy of a current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck or other government document with your name and address
No ID with you? You can still vote. Election officers check your registration, and once it's confirmed you sign an Affidavit of Affirmation of Voter Identity.

Who Can Vote by Mail
You can vote absentee in Delaware if you can't get to your polling place on Election Day because you:
work in public service for the United States or Delaware, or live outside the country for a while, or are the spouse or dependent living with someone who does
serve in the armed forces or merchant marine, or with the American Red Cross or USO alongside the armed forces
can't get there because of your job, including caring at home for a parent, spouse or child who needs constant care
are sick or physically disabled
are away on vacation
can't vote at that time or on that day because of your religion
are covered by the federal military and overseas voting law (UOCAVA) or another federal law
Check that your information on the ballot envelope is right, sign the oath on it, and seal it.
Hand it in at your county Department of Elections office during business hours, by 8 p.m. on Election Day. Early voting sites and polling places can't take absentee ballots.
Forgot to sign the oath or seal the envelope? The Department will try to reach you by phone or email so you can fix it. It counts if you fix it before the polls close at 8 p.m. on Election Day.

Questions People Ask
Answers come from official Delaware sources. Last checked October 25, 2026.
When are polls open in Delaware on Election Day?
Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., and you vote at your assigned polling place.
From the Delaware Department of Elections .
Do I need ID to vote in Delaware?
Yes. Show proof of identity, such as a photo ID, a utility bill, or a bank statement. If you don't have it with you, you can sign an affidavit and vote.
From the Delaware Code .
Can I still register to vote in Delaware?
No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 10, 2026, and Delaware has no same-day registration.
From the Delaware Department of Elections .
When is the mail ballot deadline in Delaware?
Your county Department of Elections office must receive it by 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. A postmark isn't enough, so mail it as soon as you can, or hand it in at your county office.
From the Delaware Department of Elections .
What is a provisional ballot in Delaware, and does it count?
If your eligibility can't be confirmed at the polls, you can vote a provisional ballot for the federal offices on the ballot. The Department reviews provisional ballots the day after the election.
From the Delaware Code .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Delaware?
You can still vote. Anyone waiting in line by 8 p.m. on Election Day, inside or outside the voting room, is allowed to vote.
From the Delaware Code .
How do I check that my mail ballot was counted in Delaware?
Check your absentee ballot's status on iVote, the state's voter portal, or call the Department of Elections.
From the Delaware Department of Elections .
What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in Delaware?
The Department will try to reach you by phone or email. Sign the oath, or seal the envelope, before the polls close at 8 p.m. on Election Day and your ballot counts.
From the Delaware Code .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in Delaware?
It has to reach your county office by 8 p.m. on November 3, and a postmark doesn't count. In the last days, take it to your county Department of Elections office instead.
From the Delaware Department of Elections .

Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
