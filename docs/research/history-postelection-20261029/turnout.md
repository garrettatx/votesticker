## Answer, Summarized Item by Item (quotes as Perplexity gave them)

Headline: no study randomly gave or withheld a paper "I Voted" sticker and measured validated turnout. The
popular claim that stickers raise turnout has no direct study behind it.

1. **Kraitzman, DeMora and Albarracín (2025)**, Political Communication 42(6):951-967, doi 10.1080/10584609.2025.2472768.
   Two preregistered experiments, N = 1,500 low-propensity U.S. adults. "I Will Vote" vs "I Voted" vs "Buckle Up"
   control stickers; measures intention, not turnout. Article also notes "the difference between the 'I Voted' and
   the 'I Will Vote' stickers is not statistically significant." (Already on the home page FAQ, pre-election only.)
2. **Vote.org 2018 SMS program, Memo 004: Digital Sticker vs Standard Practice.** Randomized; texts linking to a
   digital "I Voted" sticker for Facebook vs the standard text. "The Standard Practice treatment generated an
   increase in turnout of 0.24 percentage points ..." "The Digital Sticker treatment generated an increase in
   turnout of 0.25 percentage points ..." "Both treatments generated significant increases in turnout, and the
   size of the treatment effects was indistinguishable." Gray literature; sample (~714,000 young people) not verified.
3. **Bond et al. (2012)**, Nature 489:295-298. 61,279,316 users; validated turnout +0.39% for the social message;
   informational message 0.00%; 340,000 total estimated. (On the page already.)
4. **Jones, Bond, Bakshy, Eckles and Fowler (2017)**, PLOS ONE 12(4):e0173851. 2012 replication; adjusted +0.24%
   (95% CI +0.03 to +0.44), unadjusted +0.17% not significant; about 270,000 total.
5. **Bond et al. (2017)**, Political Communication 34:261-281: secondary analysis of 2010; not independent.
6. **Gerber, Green and Larimer (2008)**, APSR 102(1):33-48. Civic duty +1.8, Hawthorne +2.5, Self +4.8, Neighbors
   +8.1 points. (On the page already.)
7. **Panagopoulos (2010)**, Political Behavior 32:369-386: pride and shame (names in the newspaper).
8. **Mann (2010)**, Political Behavior 32:387-407: backlash to social pressure.
9. **Davenport (2010)**: face-to-face feedback with voting histories, Boston public housing.
10. **Funk (2010)**, JEEA 8(5):1077-1103: Swiss postal voting; "In spite of the large reduction in voting costs,
    the effect on aggregate turnout was small." "However, voter participation was more negatively affected in the
    smaller communities." (Visibility lost with mail voting; natural experiment.)
11. **Rogers, Ternovski and Yoeli (2016)**, PNAS 113(19):5218-5220: possible follow-up, +0.72 vs +0.48 points.
12. **DellaVigna, List, Malmendier and Rao (2017)**, REStud 84(1):143-181: "Voting to Tell Others", value about
    $15, about 2 points of turnout (model-based).
13. **Gerber, Huber, Fang and Gooch (2017)**, American Politics Research 45(4):533-559: Self mailer +0.7 points in 2014.
