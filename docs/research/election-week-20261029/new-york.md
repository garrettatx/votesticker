## Overlooked election-week facts

All sources in this section are official `.gov` sources. Quotations preserve the source wording, with PDF line breaks joined; calendar entries are explicitly identified where the source does not provide a prose sentence.

### Missing names and affidavit ballots

**1. A missing name does not automatically mean you cannot vote.** NYC’s official 2026 rights sheet expressly identifies an affidavit ballot—the term New York uses for this provisional voting route. [nyc](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/civicengagement/downloads/pdf/know_your_rights_2026.pdf)

**Exact source sentence:**
> “Vote by "affidavit ballot” if your name is missing from the list of voters at your polling site.” [nyc](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/civicengagement/downloads/pdf/know_your_rights_2026.pdf)

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.nyc.gov/assets/civicengagement/downloads/pdf/know_your_rights_2026.pdf](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/civicengagement/downloads/pdf/know_your_rights_2026.pdf)

**2. First check that you are at the correct table; a court order is another possible remedy.** Ulster’s election-office FAQ gives both steps. [elections.ulstercountyny](https://elections.ulstercountyny.gov/frequently-asked-questions/)

**Exact quoted sentences:**
> “If you are not on the poll-list, ask the inspector to verify that you are at the proper table for your address.”

> “You can obtain a court order or complete an affidavit ballot.” [elections.ulstercountyny](https://elections.ulstercountyny.gov/frequently-asked-questions/)

**Official URL:**  
[https://elections.ulstercountyny.gov/frequently-asked-questions/](https://elections.ulstercountyny.gov/frequently-asked-questions/)

**3. A move within New York does not necessarily require forfeiting this election.** The state’s guidance addresses a registered voter who moved, has lived at the new address for at least 30 days, and forgot to update the address. [elections.ny](https://elections.ny.gov/voting-after-incarceration)

**Exact quoted sentences:**
> “If you’ve lived at your new address for 30+ days, go to your new polling place.”

> “You’ll be given an “affidavit ballot” which will update and transfer your voter registration and allow your vote to be counted if you are otherwise eligible.” [elections.ny](https://elections.ny.gov/voting-after-incarceration)

**Official URL:**  
[https://elections.ny.gov/voting-after-incarceration](https://elections.ny.gov/voting-after-incarceration)

**4. Affidavit ballots have a tracking requirement—not just mail ballots.** State law requires election boards to enter information allowing voters to determine whether these votes counted. [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Exact quoted sentence:**
> “The board of elections shall enter information into the ballot tracking system, as defined in sections 8-414 and 8-712 of this chapter, to allow a voter who cast a ballot in an early voting or affidavit envelope to determine if the vote was counted.” [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Official law URL, §9-209(7)(l):**  
[https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Where to check:** [https://voterlookup.elections.ny.gov/](https://voterlookup.elections.ny.gov/) — Fulton County’s official explanation specifically says this tracking tool shows whether an absentee or affidavit ballot counted. [fultoncountyny](https://www.fultoncountyny.gov/board-elections)

### Cures and counting

**9. A ballot problem may still be fixable after Election Day, and the cure deadline depends on when the notice was mailed.** Do not automatically describe November 10 as every voter’s final cure deadline. [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Exact quoted sentence:**
> “Such cure affirmation shall be received by the board no later than seven business days after the board's mailing of such curable rejection notice or by five p.m. on the seventh day following the election, whichever is later.” [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Official URL, §9-209(3)(e):**  
[https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**10. Electronic cures have a midnight rule.** The same statute distinguishes timely electronic submission from the deadline wording above. [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Exact quoted sentence:**
> “Any cure affirmation received electronically as an email attachment or by upload prior to midnight of the last day to cure is timely.” [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**11. Affidavit ballots receive post-election review—not necessarily an election-night count.** The statutory review period is within four business days. [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Exact quoted sentence:**
> “Within four business days of the election, the board of elections shall review all affidavit ballots cast in the election.” [nysenate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

**Official URL, §9-209(7)(a):**  
[https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/9-209)

[ontariocountyny](https://www.ontariocountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/64469/GE26-Ontario-County-NOTICE-to-Party-Chairs-and-Candidates)

**Exact quoted sentence:**
> “On or after Election Day, the canvass will be conducted within one day of receipt.” [ontariocountyny](https://www.ontariocountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/64469/GE26-Ontario-County-NOTICE-to-Party-Chairs-and-Candidates)

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.ontariocountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/64469/GE26-Ontario-County-NOTICE-to-Party-Chairs-and-Candidates](https://www.ontariocountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/64469/GE26-Ontario-County-NOTICE-to-Party-Chairs-and-Candidates)

### Canvass and certification dates

**13. November 18 and November 28 are post-election calendar milestones—not reasons to expect final results on November 4.** Tompkins County’s 2026 explanation identifies recanvass/audit milestones followed by county certification and transmission. [tompkinscountyny](https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/News-articles/Local-Elections-Basics-3-How-ballots-are-secured-and-counted)

**Exact calendar entry—not a prose sentence:**
> “Nov. 18 and Nov. 28: Recanvass and audit dates, followed by the County certification and canvass transmission date in the State political calendar.” [tompkinscountyny](https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/News-articles/Local-Elections-Basics-3-How-ballots-are-secured-and-counted)

**Official URL:**  
[https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/News-articles/Local-Elections-Basics-3-How-ballots-are-secured-and-counted](https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/News-articles/Local-Elections-Basics-3-How-ballots-are-secured-and-counted)

**Publication caution:** Describe these as the **listed calendar milestones**, not as an announcement that every county will hold its certification meeting on precisely November 28.

**14. The state canvass has a December timetable.** In a nonpresidential year such as 2026, the statutory meeting deadline is December 15; the statute also permits adjournment, so “all certification must finish December 15” would overstate this provision. [electionindex](https://electionindex.org/election/us-midterms-2026-ny)

**Exact quoted sentences:**
> “The state board of canvassers shall meet on or before the fifteenth day, or, in a year when electors of president and vice-president are chosen on or before the first Monday after the first Wednesday, of December next after each general election, and within forty days after each special election, to canvass such statements.”

> “The board may adjourn from day to day, not exceeding a term of five days.”
