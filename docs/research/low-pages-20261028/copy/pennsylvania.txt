Where You Can Vote
Want to vote before Election Day? Once your county's ballots are ready, go to your county election office, apply for a mail ballot, fill it out and hand it in, all in one visit. Bring your PennDOT ID number or the last four digits of your Social Security number. Call or check your county's website first to make sure ballots are ready.
On Election Day, go to your own polling place. At the wrong one, you can only vote a provisional ballot, and only if you believe you're eligible there.
Voting at your polling place for the first time, or at a new one after a move? Look up your polling place and bring an ID. It doesn't have to have a photo.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Pennsylvania driver's license or PennDOT ID card
ID from any Pennsylvania state agency or the U.S. government
U.S. passport or U.S. Armed Forces ID
Student ID, including a digital one
Employee ID
A voter registration card from your county
Firearm permit
Current utility bill or bank statement
Paycheck or government check
Only first-time voters at a polling place need ID. A photo ID can't be expired, canceled or revoked. An ID without a photo needs your name and address on it.
Asked for ID and can't show one? You can vote a provisional ballot, and your county decides within 7 days after the election whether it counts.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered voter can vote by mail, and you don't need a reason. Apply online, by mail or at your county election office.
Seal your ballot in the yellow envelope, put that in the outer return envelope, then sign and date the declaration on the outside. Skip the yellow envelope or the signature and date, and your ballot won't count.
Return it by mail, at a drop box, at your county election office or at another official location your county lists. You have to return your own ballot, unless you have a disability and named someone in writing to deliver it.
Each county decides whether you can fix a mistake on a returned envelope. Ask your county election office. If yours can't be fixed, you can still vote a provisional ballot at your polling place on Election Day.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
You can wear a shirt, button or hat for a candidate when you go in to vote. Inside, you can't campaign or ask anyone to back a candidate. Campaign workers and signs stay at least 10 feet from the voting room entrance.
Polls close at 8 p.m. If you're in line by then, stay in line. You can vote.

When are polls open in Pennsylvania on Election Day?

From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
Can I still register to vote in Pennsylvania?
No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 19, 2026, and Pennsylvania has no Election Day registration.

When is the mail ballot deadline in Pennsylvania?
Your county election office must have it in hand by 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3. A postmark by Election Day isn't enough, so return it as soon as you can. Overseas and military deadlines differ.
From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
What is a provisional ballot in Pennsylvania, and does it count?
It's a ballot you vote when your eligibility isn't clear at the polls, for example if your name isn't in the poll book. Your county decides within 7 days after the election, and you can look up whether it counted starting 7 days after.
From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Pennsylvania?
You can still vote. If you're in line by 8 p.m., you'll be allowed to cast your ballot.
From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
How do I check that my mail ballot was counted in Pennsylvania?
Check your ballot status online with the Department of State. If you gave your county an email address, you may also get an email when your ballot arrives.
From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in Pennsylvania?
Check before you send it: if the outer envelope isn't signed and dated, your ballot won't be counted. Questions? Call your county election office.
From the Pennsylvania Department of State .
