Drop Boxes and Voting Centers
Your ballot comes to you by mail. Rather vote in person? Go to a voter service center in your county, open from October 20 through Election Day (closed Sundays), for in-person voting, accessible voting and same day registration.
Voter service centers and ballot drop boxes are listed by county on the Office of Elections site. Drop your ballot at any drop box in your county.
Not registered on Election Day? You can still register at a voter service center in your county and vote in person. You'll get a mail ballot for future elections.

Returning Your Ballot
Every registered voter gets a ballot in the mail, no request needed. Ballots were mailed to arrive by Friday, October 16. Didn't get yours, or made a mistake? Ask your County Elections Division for a replacement.
Read the affirmation statement and sign the return envelope. Your ballot can't be counted without your signature. Can't sign on your own? Someone you trust can be your witness, but not your employer or union agent.
Drop it at any ballot drop box in your county by 7 p.m. on Election Day. Locations and hours are on the Office of Elections site.
Your county compares the signature on your envelope with the one on file. If they can't confirm a match, they'll notify you, and you have five business days after Election Day to fix it.

Questions People Ask
Answers come from official Hawaii sources. Last checked October 21, 2026.
When is the ballot deadline in Hawaii?
Hawaii votes by mail. Your ballot must be received by 7 p.m. on November 3, and a postmark isn't enough. Voter service centers are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. that day for in-person voting.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
Do I need ID to vote in Hawaii?
Usually not. At a voter service center, a clerk may ask you to confirm who you are, for example with a photo ID.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
Can I still register to vote in Hawaii?
Yes. You can register and vote the same day at a voter service center, open October 20 through November 3.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
What is a provisional ballot in Hawaii, and does it count?
You can ask for a provisional ballot if your voter registration can't be confirmed. It's counted only if your County Elections Division confirms your registration. You can find out whether it counted by calling them or on the Office of Elections website.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Hawaii?
Stay in line. If you're in line at 7 p.m. at a voter service center or a drop box, you'll be allowed to vote or drop off your ballot.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
How do I check that my mail ballot was counted in Hawaii?
Sign up for ballot tracking alerts by text, email or voice call at elections.hawaii.gov. You'll hear when your ballot is mailed, when it's received and accepted, and when the deadline is close.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
What if I forgot to sign my ballot envelope in Hawaii?
Your ballot can't be counted without your signature. If officials can't confirm your signature, they'll notify you, and you have five business days after Election Day to fix it.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in Hawaii?
It won't count. Ballots must be received, not postmarked, by 7 p.m. on November 3. If it's getting close, use a ballot drop box instead of the mail.
From the Hawaii Office of Elections .
How do I make it my profile picture?
Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
