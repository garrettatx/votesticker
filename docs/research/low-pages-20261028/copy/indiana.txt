Vote Early
Early voting in Indiana runs Tuesday, October 6 to Monday, November 2. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
Vote early at your county clerk's office or a satellite site. It ends at noon Monday, November 2, and counties set days and hours.
Election Day hours: Polls are open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. local time. Indiana has two time zones.
ID: Yes. Show a current photo ID issued by Indiana or the U.S. government, such as a driver's license or a U.S. passport. Without one, you vote a provisional ballot and have until noon 10 days after the election to follow up with your county election board.

Where You Can Vote
Vote early in person at your circuit court clerk's office or a satellite office, no reason needed. On Election Day, polls are open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. local time.
Look up your polling place at IndianaVoters.in.gov. It also shows your registration, your county election office and who's on your ballot. Your county lists its early voting dates, times and sites there too.
Registered by mail and voting for the first time? The poll list may say you need to show proof of where you live. If you can't before polls close, you vote a provisional ballot.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Indiana driver's license
Indiana photo ID card
Interim ID from the BMV
Military ID
U.S. passport
Your ID has to be current or have expired after November 5, 2024, and it must come from Indiana or the U.S. government. School and college IDs no longer count.
No ID that meets the rules? You can vote a provisional ballot. Then you have until noon 10 days after the election to bring the ID to your county election board, or to sign an affidavit that an exemption applies to you.

Who Can Vote by Mail
You can vote absentee by mail in Indiana if you can mark and sign your own ballot and you:
expect to be out of your county for all 12 hours the polls are open (6 a.m. to 6 p.m.)
have a disability
are 65 or older
have official election duties outside your precinct
are scheduled to work at your regular job for all 12 hours the polls are open
will be confined by illness or injury, or caring for someone who is, for all 12 hours the polls are open
can't vote because of a religious discipline or holiday during all 12 hours the polls are open
are in the state's address confidentiality program
are in the military or a public safety officer
have no transportation to the polls
Mark your ballot yourself and sign the ballot security envelope. No one can help you mark a mail ballot, but someone other than your employer or union representative can help you seal the envelope if they fill out the affidavit of assistance.
You can hand-deliver your finished ballot to your county election board, including at any early voting site, by 6 p.m. on Election Day. Only you, a household or family member, your attorney-in-fact or a bonded courier can drop it off, and anyone but you fills out an ABS-19 affidavit.
Forgot to sign your envelope, made a mistake or got the wrong ballot? Before Election Day, file an ABS-5 form with your county election officials to fix it.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign shirts, buttons and signs at home. Clothing or buttons naming a party, a candidate or an elected official, or backing a ballot question, count as electioneering inside the polls and the 50-foot chute leading in. If you're asked to cover up and don't, you still get to vote.
You can use your phone at the polls, as long as you don't campaign or have a loud, disruptive conversation.
Polls close at 6 p.m. local time. If you're in line by then, stay put. You'll be allowed to vote a regular ballot.

Answers come from official Indiana sources. Last checked October 26, 2026.
When are polls open in Indiana on Election Day?
Polls are open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. local time. Indiana has two time zones.
From the Indiana Election Division .
Do I need ID to vote in Indiana?
Yes. Show a current photo ID issued by Indiana or the U.S. government, such as a driver's license or a U.S. passport. Without one, you vote a provisional ballot and have until noon 10 days after the election to follow up with your county election board.
From the Indiana Election Division .
Can I still register to vote in Indiana?
No, not for the November 3 election. The deadline was October 5, 2026, and Indiana has no Election Day registration.
From the Indiana Election Division .
When is the mail ballot deadline in Indiana?
County election officials must have it by 6 p.m. local time on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. A postmark isn't enough, even one dated on or before Election Day.
From the Indiana Election Division .
What is a provisional ballot in Indiana, and does it count?
You may get one if you don't have the required photo ID or your eligibility at the precinct is challenged. After Election Day, the county election board decides whether it counts. Without ID, you have until noon 10 days after the election to follow up.
From the Indiana Election Division .
What if I am in line when the polls close in Indiana?
You can still vote. Everyone in line when polls close at 6 p.m. local time gets to vote a regular ballot.
From the Indiana Election Division .

Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
