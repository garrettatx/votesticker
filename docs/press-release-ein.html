VoteSticker.org Traces the "I Voted" Sticker Back to 1920 Paper Tags

Austin-built, nonpartisan site pairs the sticker's documented history with free digital stickers for every state

AUSTIN, TX, UNITED STATES, [publication date] /EINPresswire.com/ -- The "I voted" sticker is usually dated to the 1980s. VoteSticker.org, a free, nonpartisan site built by Austin SEO consultancy Garrett Digital, has traced "I have voted" tags back to October 1920, when the print shop at Blewett, a St. Louis public school, made them for a student election and gave one to each student as they voted.

The site's history page lays out the record with a source for each entry: a 1962 Camp Fire Girls handbook that suggested handing out tags on Election Day, a 1968 U.S. Commission on Civil Rights report describing tags pinned on voters leaving a Georgia polling place in 1966, a 1985 Phoenix Board of Realtors campaign, and the 1987 waving-flag oval designed by Janet Boudreau of Intab. It also says what is still unknown, including who first printed an adhesive "I voted" sticker.

"I built VoteSticker.org because getting an 'I voted' sticker is a small thing people enjoy," said Garrett Nafzinger, founder of Garrett Digital and creator of VoteSticker.org. "I wanted to make it easy to find one you like and share it online. And if someone still needs voting information, the site points them to official sources."

The site offers free digital "I voted," "I voted by mail" and first-time voter stickers for all 50 states and Washington, D.C., in English and Spanish, with no account, no email address and nothing for sale. Each is sized for circular profile pictures, and a transparent version can be layered over a photo or added to a story. Custom stickers are made in the visitor's browser and never uploaded to the site.

State pages cover each state's 2026 official sticker designs and contest winners, with links to official ballot tracking and election office pages. VoteSticker.org is not a government website, and its stickers are not official election-office designs or proof of voting.
