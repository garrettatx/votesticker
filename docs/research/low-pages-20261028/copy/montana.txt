When, Where, and What to Bring
Vote Early
In Montana, you can vote an absentee ballot in person at your county election office from Monday, October 5, until noon on Monday, November 2. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
You can apply and pick up your ballot in the same visit. Office hours vary by county, so check yours before you go.
Election Day hours: Most polling places are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. A polling place with fewer than 400 voters may open as late as noon, and the county mails a notice if it does.
ID: Yes. Show a photo ID such as a Montana driver's license, a state ID card, a U.S. passport, or a tribal photo ID. Another photo ID works if you also bring a utility bill or bank statement with your name and address.

Where You Can Vote
Want to vote before Election Day? Go to your county election office, apply for an absentee ballot, and mark it right there. Ballots are ready for in-person voting 30 days before Election Day. On Election Day, vote at your polling place.
Your county election office sets your polling place. Look it up, along with your sample ballot, in the Secretary of State's voter lookup.

What ID to Bring
Bring one of these:
Driver's license or REAL ID
Montana state ID card
Military ID
Tribal photo ID
U.S. passport
Montana concealed carry permit
Montana University System or NAIA school photo ID
Your ID has to be current, valid and readable.
Have a different photo ID with your name? Bring it plus a current utility bill, bank statement, paycheck, government check or other government document with your name and current address. Without either, you vote a provisional ballot and have until 5 p.m. on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, to show ID.

Who Can Vote by Mail
Any registered voter in Montana can vote absentee, no reason needed. You can also ask to get an absentee ballot mailed for every future election.
Put your ballot in the secrecy envelope, then in the return envelope. Sign and date the affirmation on the return envelope yourself. It also has a space for your birth year, which isn't being enforced right now.
Return it by mail, or drop it off at your county election office or any polling place in your county.
Ballot rejected? Fill out the elector signature part of the Fix My Rejected Ballot form with your signature, today's date and your birth year, add a copy of an acceptable ID, and return it to your county election office.

Phones, Photos, and Campaign Gear
Leave the campaign buttons and badges at home. Montana bans wearing or showing any badge, button or insignia for a candidate or ballot issue where you vote, and bans electioneering within 100 feet of the building entrance.
In line at 8 p.m. at your polling place or the election office? Stay in line. You can still vote.

Questions People Ask
Answers come from official Montana sources. Last checked October 26, 2026.
When are polls open in Montana on Election Day?
Most polling places are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. A polling place with fewer than 400 voters may open as late as noon, and the county mails a notice if it does.
From the Montana Code .
Do I need ID to vote in Montana?
Yes. Show a photo ID such as a Montana driver's license, a state ID card, a U.S. passport, or a tribal photo ID. Another photo ID works if you also bring a utility bill or bank statement with your name and address.
From the Montana Code .
Can I still register to vote in Montana?
Yes, in person. Regular registration closed October 5, 2026. You can still register late at your county election office and vote there; ask your county for the cutoff, because a court has blocked the 2025 law that set one.
From the Montana Code .
Can I take a picture of my ballot in Montana?
Yes. Montana's Commissioner of Political Practices says a selfie with your own marked ballot, shared with friends or on social media, doesn't break state law.
From politicalpractices.mt.gov .
When is the mail ballot deadline in Montana?
Your ballot must reach your county election office or a polling place by 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Mailing it? Send it at least a week before Election Day.
From the Montana Secretary of State .
What is a provisional ballot in Montana, and does it count?
You vote a provisional ballot if you can't show the required ID, your name isn't in the register, or your registration can't be confirmed at the polls. Bring ID or eligibility information to your county by 5 p.m. the day after the election, and it counts once your county verifies you.
From the Montana Code .
How do I check that my mail ballot was counted in Montana?
Use the Secretary of State's Track My Ballot tool to see whether your county election office has accepted your ballot.
From VoteMT.gov (Montana Secretary of State) .
Is it too late to mail my ballot in Montana?
Mailing it in the last week is risky. It has to reach your county election office or a polling place by 8 p.m. on Election Day, so drop it off in person instead.
From VoteMT.gov (Montana Secretary of State) .
How do I make it my profile picture?
Save your sticker (Save to Photos on iPhone, Download picture elsewhere), then open your profile settings in the app and choose the saved image as your photo. The design stays inside the circle that profile photos are cropped to.
When should I switch to I voted?
After you vote. Under What should it say?, choose I voted, or I voted early or I voted by mail if that fits.
Is this an official election site?
No. Vote Sticker is an independent, nonpartisan tool made by one person. For registration, polling places, and deadlines, go to your state or local election office. Each state page links to it.
Where do the dates come from?
From official state sources: the election office's own pages or state law, checked and dated. Until a state's dates are confirmed, its stickers say Election Day.
Why do some states say hours or start dates vary?
In some states, counties or towns set their own early voting days, hours, and locations. Your local election office has the final details.
What if my state doesn't have early voting?
Your sticker says VOTE NOV 3. If you can't vote in person on Election Day, you may qualify for an absentee ballot. Your state's page links to the rules.
Do you keep my information?
Your sticker is made in your browser and never uploaded, and the site never asks for a photo, a name or an email address. Google Analytics and Ahrefs Web Analytics count visits; the About page lists exactly what they record.
